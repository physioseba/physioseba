What it means

Rehabilitation that prepares you for real sporting demands

Sports physiotherapy is more than reducing pain. It aims to restore the movement, strength, control, endurance and confidence needed for your chosen activity. Treatment is adapted to the diagnosis, healing stage, training history, position or event, and the equipment available at home.

01

Assessment before assumptions

Similar symptoms can come from different tissues and movement problems. We screen first, identify what is safe to test and refer for medical review or imaging when findings suggest it may change care.

02

Progressive, measurable loading

Exercises are selected for a reason and progressed using symptoms, technique, strength, workload and functional milestones—not simply the number of days since injury.

03

Return with criteria, not guesswork

Where appropriate, later rehabilitation includes running, jumping, cutting, throwing or other sport-specific preparation before unrestricted participation.

Personalised care

What your sports rehabilitation may include

Evidence-informed care typically combines education and active rehabilitation. The exact mix is chosen after assessment and adjusted as you improve.

  • Activity modification and practical load management
  • Range-of-motion and mobility work where indicated
  • Progressive muscle and tendon strengthening
  • Balance, proprioception and movement-control training
  • Running, landing, change-of-direction or throwing progression
  • Warm-up, recovery and injury-risk reduction guidance
  • A realistic home programme with clear dosage
  • Communication with your physician or surgeon when needed

Active rehabilitation comes first

Hands-on techniques or symptom-relief modalities may sometimes support care, but they do not replace progressive exercise, education and preparation for the demands you want to return to.

No generic promises: we do not promise a fixed recovery date or guaranteed cure. Your response and objective milestones guide the plan.

The pathway

Four phases from injury to confident return

PHASE 1

Protect & understand

Screen severity, settle highly irritable symptoms, maintain safe movement and explain what to do—and what to avoid.

PHASE 2

Restore capacity

Progress mobility, strength, balance and tolerance while monitoring the response during and after activity.

PHASE 3

Rebuild performance

Add speed, power, endurance and sport-specific patterns based on the athlete’s level and environment.

PHASE 4

Return & reduce risk

Use functional criteria, confidence, workload planning and a maintenance strategy to support return.

People also ask

Sports injury physiotherapy FAQs

1. What is sports injury physiotherapy?

It is assessment-led rehabilitation that helps restore movement, strength, capacity and confidence after a sports or exercise injury.

2. Do you provide sports physiotherapy at home in Dhaka?

Yes. Physio Seba provides home visits across Dhaka, subject to therapist availability and clinical suitability.

3. Which sports injuries can physiotherapy help?

Common examples include sprains, muscle strains, tendinopathy, ligament or meniscus rehabilitation, shoulder injuries and post-operative recovery.

4. When should I start physiotherapy after an injury?

Timing depends on the injury. Seek an assessment early; deformity, inability to bear weight, severe swelling or worsening neurological symptoms need medical evaluation first.

5. How long does sports injury rehabilitation take?

There is no fixed timeline. It varies with diagnosis, severity, healing, sport demands, health, consistency and response.

6. Will physiotherapy be painful?

Some exercises may feel challenging, but care should be adjusted to an acceptable response and should not rely on forcing severe pain.

7. Do I need an MRI before physiotherapy?

Not always. Many injuries can first be assessed clinically; imaging or referral is advised when findings may change management.

8. Can you help me return to football or cricket?

Yes. Where appropriate, rehabilitation can progress toward running, cutting, jumping, kicking, throwing or other sport-specific demands.

9. What happens at the first visit?

The physiotherapist reviews your history and goals, checks movement and function, screens referral needs, explains findings and agrees on a plan.

10. Do you treat ACL injuries?

Physiotherapy is important before and after many ACL procedures and in selected non-operative pathways, coordinated with the treating specialist when required.

11. Can physiotherapy help an ankle sprain?

Yes. Rehabilitation may address swelling, mobility, strength, balance and graded return after serious injury has been excluded.

12. Can you treat a hamstring strain?

Physiotherapy may use progressive loading, strength and running exposure based on injury irritability and functional milestones.

13. Do you treat tennis elbow?

Yes. Management commonly includes education, activity modification and progressive forearm loading after assessment.

14. Can physiotherapy help shoulder sports injuries?

Yes, depending on diagnosis. Care may address mobility, rotator-cuff and shoulder-blade capacity, and sport-specific load.

15. Is home physiotherapy suitable for every sports injury?

No. Some injuries need imaging, medical review, surgery, emergency care or equipment not appropriate for home treatment.

16. How many sessions will I need?

The number is individual. Progress should be reviewed using measurable goals rather than promising a standard package for every injury.

17. What should I wear?

Wear comfortable clothing that allows the injured area and relevant movements to be assessed while maintaining privacy.

18. Should I stop all exercise while injured?

Not necessarily. Relative rest and modified training may be more useful than complete inactivity, but advice must match your diagnosis.

19. Do you provide exercises between visits?

Yes. A clear, manageable home programme is usually part of rehabilitation and is progressed according to response.

20. How is progress measured?

Measures may include pain response, range, strength, balance, hop or functional tests, workload tolerance and confidence.

21. What is return-to-sport testing?

It is a criteria-based review of physical capacity, symptoms, sport demands and confidence used to support a safer return decision.

22. Can you guarantee a complete cure?

No ethical provider can guarantee an outcome. Recovery depends on the injury and personal factors; we provide an individual plan and monitor response.

23. Do you use machines for sports injuries?

Treatment follows assessment. Exercise, education and progressive loading are central; adjuncts are used only when appropriate.

24. Can you visit my gym or training ground?

Ask when booking. Visits depend on location, therapist availability and whether the environment is suitable.

25. Do you serve all areas of Dhaka?

We cover many areas including Dhanmondi, Mirpur, Uttara, Gulshan, Banani, Bashundhara, Badda and nearby locations.

26. Are female physiotherapists available?

Male and female physiotherapists may be available. Request your preference; confirmation depends on location and schedule.

27. How much does a session cost?

Cost depends on location, duration and clinical needs. Call or WhatsApp 01914-958611 for a quote before confirmation.

28. Can older recreational athletes receive rehabilitation?

Yes. The plan is adapted to health, capacity, goals and risk factors rather than age alone.

29. What if pain becomes worse during rehabilitation?

Stop the aggravating activity and contact your physiotherapist. Sudden severe pain, major swelling, breathlessness, chest pain or neurological symptoms need urgent care.
