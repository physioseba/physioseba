Understanding Stroke Recovery

What Is Stroke Rehabilitation Physiotherapy?

Stroke rehabilitation physiotherapy is a structured program of movement-based therapy that helps the brain and body relearn lost function after a stroke (cerebrovascular accident). It focuses on rebuilding strength, balance, coordination and daily-life independence through repeated, guided practice.

How a Stroke Affects Movement and Independence

A stroke can cause one-sided weakness (hemiparesis) or paralysis (hemiplegia), muscle stiffness (spasticity), and loss of balance and coordination. This often makes everyday tasks — walking, sitting up, dressing, transferring from bed to chair — difficult or unsafe without support.

Why Early Physiotherapy Matters

The brain has the ability to reorganize and form new connections after injury — a process called neuroplasticity. Starting guided physiotherapy as soon as your physician confirms the patient is medically stable can support this recovery window. Always follow your treating doctor's clearance before beginning therapy.

Recovery Journey

Recovery Timeline & What to Expect

Recovery pace depends on stroke severity, the area of the brain affected, and overall health. This general framework helps families understand what each phase typically involves — your physiotherapist will set realistic goals for your specific case.

  1. 1

    Acute / Early Phase

    Focus on safe positioning, gentle movement, preventing complications like stiffness or pressure sores, and beginning basic mobility work once medically cleared.

  2. 2

    Sub-Acute / Intensive Recovery Phase

    More active, structured sessions targeting strength, balance, gait and hand function, alongside caregiver training for daily tasks.

  3. 3

    Chronic / Maintenance Phase

    Ongoing exercise, fall-prevention strategies and independence-building to protect gains and support long-term quality of life.

When to Book

Signs You or a Loved One May Need Stroke Physiotherapy

  • Weakness or heaviness on one side of the body
  • Difficulty walking, standing or keeping balance
  • Trouble with transfers (bed to chair, sitting to standing)
  • Muscle stiffness or spasms in the affected arm or leg
  • Recent falls or fear of falling
  • Reduced ability to manage daily self-care tasks
If you notice sudden new weakness, facial drooping, slurred speech, or confusion, this may be an active stroke — seek emergency medical care immediately. This page is for planned rehabilitation after medical stabilization, not emergency guidance.

Common Questions

Frequently Asked Questions About Stroke Rehabilitation Physiotherapy

What is stroke rehabilitation physiotherapy?

It's a structured physiotherapy program that helps stroke patients regain movement, strength, balance and independence through guided, repeated exercise and hands-on techniques.

How soon after a stroke should physiotherapy start?

Timing depends on medical stability and is decided by the treating physician. Once cleared, starting structured physiotherapy promptly generally supports better recovery outcomes.

Can physiotherapy help a paralyzed arm or leg after a stroke?

Physiotherapy can help many patients regain some movement, strength and function through targeted exercises, though the degree of recovery varies by severity and individual factors.

How long does stroke rehabilitation usually take?

Recovery timelines vary widely — from weeks to many months — depending on stroke severity, the brain area affected, and consistency of therapy. Your physiotherapist will set realistic milestones for your case.

Is home physiotherapy as effective as hospital or clinic-based rehab?

The core techniques are the same as in a clinic. Home physiotherapy can also help because exercises are practiced in the patient's real living environment, which supports carryover into daily life.

What exercises are commonly used in stroke recovery?

Common approaches include balance training, gait re-education, range-of-motion exercises, strengthening, and functional task practice — all personalized by your physiotherapist to your condition.

How much does stroke rehabilitation physiotherapy cost in Dhaka?

Cost depends on the patient's condition, session frequency and location within Dhaka. Call or WhatsApp +8801914958611 for an exact quote.

Can a stroke patient walk again with physiotherapy?

Many stroke patients regain some level of walking ability with consistent gait and balance training, though outcomes depend on the severity and location of the stroke.

What is neuro-developmental treatment (NDT)?

NDT is a hands-on physiotherapy approach that uses guided movement and positioning to help retrain normal movement patterns after neurological injury such as a stroke.

Does physiotherapy help with balance problems after a stroke?

Yes. Balance and coordination training is a core part of stroke rehabilitation and also helps reduce fall risk.

What is spasticity, and can physiotherapy reduce it?

Spasticity is muscle stiffness or tightness that can follow a stroke. Physiotherapy techniques such as stretching, positioning and guided movement can help manage it as part of an overall plan with your physician.

How many physiotherapy sessions does a stroke patient typically need?

This varies by patient. After the first home assessment, your physiotherapist will recommend a personalized plan with an estimated session frequency and duration.

Can physiotherapy help a bedridden stroke patient?

Yes. Bedridden patients can benefit from positioning, gentle range-of-motion work, and bed mobility training to prevent complications and prepare for further recovery.

What happens during the first home physiotherapy assessment?

Your physiotherapist evaluates strength, movement, balance and daily-function goals, then builds a personalized treatment plan with you and your family.

Can family members be trained to assist with stroke recovery exercises?

Yes. Caregiver training is part of our approach so family members can safely support exercises and daily transfers between sessions.

Is stroke physiotherapy painful?

Sessions are generally designed to be tolerable and are paced to the patient's comfort. Some stretching or effort-based exercises may cause mild, temporary discomfort, which your physiotherapist will manage.

What equipment does the physiotherapist bring to a home visit?

Our physiotherapists bring the basic assessment and treatment equipment required for a home-based session.

Can physiotherapy prevent a second stroke?

Physiotherapy itself doesn't prevent stroke recurrence, but improved mobility, strength and activity levels can support overall health. Stroke prevention should be managed with your physician.

What is the difference between physiotherapy and occupational therapy after stroke?

Physiotherapy focuses on movement, strength and mobility, while occupational therapy focuses on daily-living skills like dressing and eating. Many stroke patients benefit from both.

How is progress tracked during stroke rehabilitation?

Your physiotherapist monitors changes in strength, balance, mobility and functional independence across sessions and adjusts the plan accordingly.

Can elderly stroke patients safely do physiotherapy at home?

Yes. Our physiotherapists are experienced with elderly and post-stroke patients, using safe, gentle techniques suited to their needs.

What should I prepare before the physiotherapist's first visit?

Have any medical reports or discharge summaries ready, along with a clear space for movement, so the assessment can start smoothly.

Does physiotherapy help with speech or swallowing after a stroke?

Speech and swallowing difficulties are typically managed by a speech-language therapist. Ask us and we can help guide you toward the right referral alongside physiotherapy.

Can physiotherapy help prevent falls after a stroke?

Yes. Balance training, strength work and home mobility guidance are core parts of reducing fall risk in stroke recovery.

We can still provide bed-based physiotherapy, including positioning, range-of-motion work and gradual mobility progression as appropriate.

How is a treatment plan personalized for each stroke patient?

Plans are based on the patient's specific weakness pattern, stage of recovery, home environment and personal goals, reassessed as progress is made.

When should I call a doctor instead of starting physiotherapy?

If you notice sudden new weakness, facial drooping, slurred speech or confusion, seek emergency medical care immediately rather than starting physiotherapy.
